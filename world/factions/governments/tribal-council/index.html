The Tribal Council

The council of Chieftains that speaks for the United Anamor Nation, the closest thing the Anamor have ever built to a government and a good deal less than one. The great clans, Titan, Dawnplains, Paledusk, and Leviathan, send their Chieftains to convene in Pinthal whenever a matter crosses the lines between their territories. There they say what their clans will commit to, settle what can be settled, and go home. Between councils, each clan rules itself entirely.

The Anamor were here before the Shattering brought the organized nations, and they built no empire because they never needed one. The council is the lightest structure that lets a people of proud, independent clans act as one when the world demands it, and refuses to be anything heavier. It does not make law. It does not command armies. It keeps the clans from one another’s throats and presents a single face to the nations that arrived after.

Overview

Section titled “Overview”
TypeFederation council of clan Chieftains
MembersThe four great clans: Titan, Dawnplains, Paledusk, Leviathan
SeatPinthal, hosted in Titan territory
Decision ruleVoluntary commitment; deadlocks broken by the roll
AuthorityMediation only. Nothing it decides is binding by force.
ScopeMatters that cross clan territory; the face Gankora shows outward

The council convenes by summons when a matter exceeds a single clan: trade with outside nations, the terms under which foreigners may travel the interior, disputes between clans that the clans cannot settle alone, and how Gankora answers the rest of the continent. Everything else, the daily life and law of a clan within its own range, stays with the clan and never reaches Pinthal.

What holds the federation together is less the council than the land itself. The four great clans hold separate territories that rarely overlap: Titan in the western interior, Dawnplains in the southern marshes, Paledusk in the far north, Leviathan on and beyond the western water. Distance keeps the peace. The council exists for the seasons when distance is not enough.

Values

Section titled “Values”
ValueWhy
FreedomEach clan’s independence is the first principle, ahead of the federation itself. The council was built to preserve clan autonomy, never to spend it. A Chieftain answers to the council for nothing that happens inside their own range.
TraditionThe hunt, the clan, the long memory of a people who watched empires arrive and outlast them. The council mediates toward resolution rather than ruling by precedent, because Anamor ways are carried in the clans, not written in a code.
Love / SacrificeTribe and family stand above nearly everything an Anamor holds. The council is that value stretched across clan lines: whatever their rivalries, the great clans are all Anamor, and Anamor blood is not to be spilled over a poor hunting season when talk can serve instead.

Philosophy

Section titled “Philosophy”

The Anamor measure a person by what they can do and what they can withstand. The same logic runs through the council. Standing is earned in the hunt and in the leading of a clan, and a Chieftain arrives at Pinthal already proven, with no need to argue their right to be heard. Strength is assumed. What the council is for is the question of what to do with it.

The council is built on a hard truth its founders understood plainly: that proud apex hunters who will not bow to one another can still be made to bow to fortune, and to the memory that they are one people. An Anamor clan would sooner bleed than kneel to a rival clan. It will, however, accept the fall of the bones, and it will hesitate to spend kin over a thing that can be talked through. These two facts are the whole foundation of the federation. Everything the council does is an arrangement to let independent clans cooperate without any of them having to submit to another.

This is why the council guards its own lightness. A council that grew into a government would become a thing worth seizing, a throne the clans would fight over, and the Anamor have seen what the organized nations do with thrones. They would rather have a fire to gather around than a crown to fight for.

How the Council Works

Section titled “How the Council Works”

The council convenes in Pinthal, summoned when a Chieftain judges a matter to reach beyond their own range. Titan hosts, a role that emerged from geography rather than rank: Pinthal sits where the outside world enters Gankora, and the Titan clan was there to receive it. Titan’s voice carries the weight of the host and no more. It does not preside over the others.

When the council meets, each Chieftain states plainly what their clan will and will not commit to. Much is resolved here without difficulty, because the clans rarely want the same ground and the council’s purpose is to find the arrangement each can accept. A commitment given at the council fire is a matter of a Chieftain’s honor and their clan’s name, and that is the only thing that binds it. The council holds no power to compel a clan that decides to walk. Where agreement cannot be reached, the matter simply stays open, and with it the old risk that two clans settle it themselves, with blood.

The Roll

Section titled “The Roll”

The council’s answer to deadlock is the roll. When two clans hold firm and neither will yield, the dispute is given to fortune rather than to a vote, which no proud Chieftain would lose gracefully. A throw of the marked bones names which Chieftain becomes the deadlock-clearer for that one matter, and that Chieftain’s ruling settles it.

The genius of the practice is in what it spares. A clan that bends to the roll has bent to chance, not to a rival, and so loses no standing in the bending. Every clan has equal odds of being the one whose word decides, which is what makes the outcome bearable to all of them. The deadlock-clearer is expected to rule for the good of the whole rather than for their own clan, and one who plainly rules for themselves wins the matter while spending the respect that makes a Chieftain worth following. The roll decides who clears the deadlock. Custom decides how they are judged for it afterward.

The Chieftains

Section titled “The Chieftains”

A Chieftain is the strongest of their clan, proven in the hunt. Among a people whose bodies are the living record of the beasts they have taken, leadership belongs to the one whose record speaks loudest: the apex hunter who has brought down the most dangerous prey and survived to carry its traits. The seat is neither inherited nor granted, only held by the one strong enough to hold it, and a Chieftain who weakens past the point of leading does not keep the title long.

This is why a Chieftain needs no mandate at the council. Their right to speak for their clan was settled in the field before they ever reached Pinthal, and the other Chieftains know exactly what it took to earn. The respect among them is the wary respect of predators who have each measured the others and found them worth taking seriously.

The Great Clans and Their Seats

Section titled “The Great Clans and Their Seats”

Four great clans hold seats at the council, each anchored to its territory and its prey. Fuller accounts live in the Gankora entry.

ClanTraitsTerritory
TitanLarge, physically dominantWestern interior; Pinthal and the trade routes
DawnplainsCrocodile and leopard traitsThe Black Marshes and Gethkal in the south
PaleduskWolf and bear traitsThe far north; Vakrul
LeviathanCrustacean, cephalopod, and shark traitsThe western water and coast; Corvesh

The countless smaller nomadic clans of Gankora are not absent from the federation, but they hold no seats of their own. Each ranges within a great clan’s territory and falls under that clan’s umbrella, and the great Chieftain speaks for all who range their land. A minor clan with a matter of its own brings it to the council by petition through its presiding great clan, which carries the question to Pinthal or declines to.

Two clans named in Anamor memory or knowledge sit outside this structure. The Dreamweavers, rumored to carry prophetic traits from some unknown bloodline, are spoken of as real and have never been confirmed by any outsider; they hold no seat and may hold no territory at all. The last major tribe, the Rewera, range far to the north in Lithywae, beyond Gankora and beyond the council’s reach. They are not of the United Anamor Nation.

GM: The Haqol Seat

There is a fifth voice at the council that no outsider is permitted to know. The Haqol sheltered in Gankora’s deep interior hold a seat in the council, speaking for their settlement in eastern Gankora, and the arrangement has stood for generations. It formalized slowly: the Anamor offer of shelter became an offer of partnership, and the Haqol, who trust concrete assurance over goodwill, pressed for a place at the fire rather than a promise.

The representative’s identity is known to the attending Chieftains and never communicated outward. Visitors to Pinthal learn nothing of it. The federation’s restriction on Highborn movement beyond the major cities did not exist before the Haqol formalized their seat, and it is no coincidence that it followed.

Polarities

Section titled “Polarities”

The Anamor are a people of Conversion, the law that lets them absorb and rewrite the body through consumed blood. A clan’s strength is the strength of what it has hunted, and the traits its Chieftain carries are the proof of a council seat earned rather than given.

The Clans by Trait

Section titled “The Clans by Trait”
  • Conversion, expressed through the prey each clan takes

There is no shared martial doctrine across the council the way the sects of Lithywae or the tracks of the Eventide Brigade teach one. Each clan hunts what its territory holds and becomes what it consumes, so a Titan fights nothing like a Leviathan and neither fights like a Dawnplains hunter raised in the marsh. What the Anamor share is the principle, not the form: the body is made by the hunt, and the hunt never ends.

History

Section titled “History”

The Anamor were among the peoples displaced when the Elkyr, Janim, and Nyl arrived in the Shattering. Their nomadic clan structure could not stand against organized nation-states, and resentment of the newcomers lingers without ever uniting into a single movement. The clans did not consolidate into an empire in answer. They consolidated only as far as survival required, into a federation that lets them face the outside world together while leaving each clan as free within its range as it was before.

The council took its present shape around Pinthal because that is where the outside world reaches Gankora first, and because the Titan clan was positioned to receive it. Its most consequential collective acts have been defensive: the restriction of Highborn movement in Gankoran territory, the control of foreign access to the interior, and the quiet, absolute refusal to let outsiders reach the people the Anamor shelter in their deep wilds.

Relations

Section titled “Relations”

The federation’s standing with each nation is detailed in the Gankora entry and summarized here.

  • Kyr’elian is Gankora’s most functional relationship. Trade flows through the southern border, and the Assembly has treated the federation as a peer and made no demands of it.
  • Lithywae is moderate, formal, and stable. Northern Paledusk communities maintain a low, old exchange with their counterparts across the Nylvish border that predates any formal relations.
  • Dosharen is poor. Dosharen’s Highborn houses have pressed repeatedly to locate the Haqol within Gankora, by diplomacy and by other means, and the federation has answered by closing its interior to Highborn and cooling on trade and exchange.
  • The Hammer Accord is a neighbor with standing. Three Okoi city-states sit in Gankoran territory, and the two native peoples deal as equals, the federation claiming no sovereignty over the city-states and the Okoi asking none of the council.
