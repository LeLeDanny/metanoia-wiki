The Hammer Accord

The confederacy of the six Okoi city-states: Kolvrath, Duvrak, Ashveth, Halvrak, Kethvar, and Thirkoth. The Accord holds no territory, commands no standing army of its own, and governs none of its members internally. What it does is coordinate the one thing the Okoi possess that the rest of the continent cannot do without, and ensure that Okoi metalwork, Okoi secrets, and Okoi independence are never used against the Okoi.

Its power rests on a single fact understood by every ruler who has ever dealt with it: no nation wants to be cut off from Okoi arms and the devices only the Okoi can build, and none wants either in the hands of their enemies. The leverage runs one direction, and the Accord has spent centuries making certain it stays that way. It is slow, consensus-bound, and impossible to buy. It exists to protect the Okoi way of life from the only thing that could ever truly end it, which is the Okoi being divided and absorbed one mountain at a time.

Overview

Section titled “Overview”
TypeConfederacy of city-state heads
MembersThe six Okoi city-states
SeatNone: convenes in the city of whoever summons it
Decision ruleConsensus. Any single city-state can block.
VoiceOne per city-state, held by its chief forge-master
LeverageOkoi metalwork and Ren-driven devices, and the secrets behind them

The Accord is a final court and a collective negotiating body, nothing more and nothing less. It convenes when a matter exceeds a single city: trade terms that touch all six, a threat too large for one city to face, or a dispute between city-states that cannot be settled locally. Outside those matters, each city-state is wholly its own authority, with its own law, its own command, and its own dealings with the nation around it.

This is deliberate. A confederacy that governed its members would be a thing worth conquering. A confederacy that only ever acts to keep the Okoi whole, and otherwise leaves each city to itself, has no center to seize and no majority to purchase.

Values

Section titled “Values”
ValueWhy
TraditionThe craft, the Names of the Mountain, the dedication of every child, the anchoring of each city to its peak. The Okoi are an old people who never left, and continuity is the spine of everything they are.
FreedomEach city-state’s independence is held to be worth more than any alliance that would compromise it, including the Accord itself. The confederacy exists to preserve that independence, never to override it.
PowerNot conquest, but leverage. The Okoi made themselves too valuable to eliminate, and the weapons on which the continent depends are the guarantee of their survival. Strength held in reserve is the point of the strength.

Philosophy

Section titled “Philosophy”

The Okoi were here before the migration and were displaced by the civilizations that arrived in it. Where the Anamor went nomadic and the Haqol went into hiding, the Okoi consolidated, dug into their mountains, and made themselves indispensable. Everything the Accord does descends from that one choice: survival through value rather than territory, leverage rather than land.

The Accord moves slowly and on purpose. Consensus and the single-city veto make it nearly impossible to push and entirely impossible to co-opt, and the Okoi consider that worth every delay it costs. They are not interested in expansion, hegemony, or the continent’s politics for their own sake. They are interested in being left alone on terms of their choosing, and in never again being a people who can be picked apart.

What unites the six is colder than affection or shared rule: the understanding that divided, they are six mountains waiting to be taken in turn, and united, they are the only smiths on the continent. To the Okoi, unity and secrecy are less virtues than the plain conditions of continued existence.

How the Accord Works

Section titled “How the Accord Works”

The Accord has no capital and no permanent hall. It convenes by summons, called by any city-state head who judges a matter to be of collective weight, and it meets in the summoning city. The Okoi have no center because the Okoi have no center: to seat the Accord permanently in one city would make that city first among the six, and the confederacy would not survive the resentment.

Decisions require consensus rather than majority. Every city-state holds one voice, and any one of them can block a collective action outright. In practice this makes the Accord deliberate to the point of glacial, and it makes external manipulation futile. No nation, house, or guild has ever split the Accord by buying a majority, because a majority decides nothing.

When the Accord does reach a decision, it functions as the final court of the Okoi: the last word on disputes between city-states and on threats no single city can answer. Below that, it has no reach. A house that leans on Thirkoth is dealing with Thirkoth, not the Accord, and learns the difference quickly.

The Seat-Holders

Section titled “The Seat-Holders”

Each city-state’s voice is held by its chief forge-master, an elected office and the paramount head of the city.

Within a city-state, day-to-day civic and military authority belongs to the officer class, a single merged command that polices the streets and holds the walls. The chief forge-master sits above that command. The forge is the city’s purpose and the sole source of the leverage that keeps the Okoi alive, so the one who masters the forge leads the city and carries its voice to the rest of the Okoi world. The office is filled by election, decided between the masters of the forge and the senior officer command, binding the two centers of a city’s power into the choice of its head.

A chief forge-master speaks for the whole of their city when the Accord convenes. That voice is singular and absolute in the moment: there is no delegation to overrule, no faction to appease at the table. It is also why the seat carries the weight it does, and why the failure of a seat-holder is treated as gravely as it is.

The Work of the Forges

Section titled “The Work of the Forges”

The Okoi reputation as the continent’s finest smiths undersells what their forges now produce. Conventional arms and armor, the swords and tools that built the reputation, are the floor of Okoi craft rather than its ceiling. The city-states are the most advanced makers of Ren-driven devices on the Remnant Continent, and they are years ahead of anyone positioned to catch them.

The Gate of Zerathrain’s celebrated exerter research, the constructed devices that channel Ren without a living wielder, is the same field the Janim are now turning toward war: hand exerters that hurl force the way a Rennim’s ability would, what plainer folk have begun to call guns. The Janim believe this will revolutionize warfare. The Okoi have been building in this direction far longer, and the breadth of what comes out of their mountains shows it:

  • Golems and automatons. Ren-driven constructs for labor, haulage, and war, the walking strength of a city that need not feed a standing garrison to wield it.
  • Engines and vehicles. Powered machines for the forge, the mine, and the road, the means by which a city dug into a mountain moves ore, water, and itself.
  • Exerter arms. The Okoi make the gun too, and make it better, though they sell it more carefully than anything else they produce.

Two Okoi devices in particular have reshaped how a fight against them unfolds, and together they read as a single answer to the age of the gun:

  • The shield. Okoi shield-devices turn aside exerter fire and fast-moving force, rendering a volley of guns close to useless against a prepared Okoi line. A people who can build the gun and negate it both has little to fear from an arms race the rest of the continent is only beginning to run.
  • The singing blade. Against a shield that stops anything quick, the Okoi answer with the slow cut. Their bladed weapons vibrate at a pitch that slips past a shield’s guard and parts flesh, stone, and lesser metal with an ease that unsettles anyone who faces it for the first time. The sound it makes in the cut is its name.

This is the true depth of the Accord’s leverage. The continent does not merely want Okoi swords. It wants what the Okoi can build that no one else can, and it fears what would follow if those devices were ever turned outward in number.

Craft Control

Section titled “Craft Control”

The Accord’s leverage is only as durable as the secrets behind it, and the Okoi guard those secrets in three layers at once.

  • The methods never leave. The Okoi sell finished weapons, tools, and devices. They do not sell the craft. The Recurrence-informed understanding of the rhythms of metal, fire, and force that makes Okoi work unmatchable is never taught beyond the city walls, and no outsider is ever taken as an apprentice to it. What a buyer receives is a result, never a means.
  • The best work stays home. The finest Okoi craft, the shield-devices, the singing blades, the war automatons, is not for sale at any price. What reaches foreign hands is fine enough to keep a nation dependent and never fine enough to be reverse-engineered into independence. The Okoi calibrate exactly how good the export grade is allowed to be.
  • Oath and the mountain. Forge-masters are bound by oath to the craft, and an Okoi who would carry the methods to outsiders is severed from the people entirely, written onto the Names of the Mountain as one who has died and left. The craft is treated as belonging to all Okoi, never to the smith who happens to hold it.

Unity, and Its Price

Section titled “Unity, and Its Price”

The Okoi hold no territory of their own. Six mountains, six cities, and the secrets of the forge are the whole of what they are, and unity and secrecy are the only things standing between them and erasure. The one outcome the Accord exists to prevent is a city-state being drawn into a host nation: annexed, made a subject, its forge turned to another flag. A people that can be absorbed one mountain at a time is a people already ending.

For this reason a seat-holder who defies a consensus decision has done far more than err locally. He has moved, whatever his intent, to doom every Okoi alive. Selling the craft, bending a city toward a host nation, or breaking a decision the six reached for their collective survival is treason against all of them, and it is judged by all of them.

Here, and only here, the Accord’s authority reaches past a city-state’s threshold. In every ordinary matter the confederacy stops at a city’s gate, but Okoi tradition is older than the Accord and is not the property of any single city. A seat-holder who breaks a consensus decision is stripped of his seat, imprisoned, and replaced, his city electing a new chief forge-master in his place. The offender’s own city is expected to deliver him, because a city that sheltered such a man would itself become the crack that breaks Okoi unity, and no Okoi will be the one to do that.

It is rarely needed. Like the inviolability of the dedication circle, the rule’s true strength is that no Okoi can imagine being the one to break it.

Polarities

Section titled “Polarities”

The Okoi are a people of Recurrence, the attunement to cycles, weather, tides, and rhythms, and the Accord’s strength grows from how that polarity expresses in two directions at once.

The Forge

Section titled “The Forge”
  • Recurrence, Metal, Heat, Earth

Okoi smithing is Recurrence made practical: the reading of the rhythms of metal, fire, and force that no outside smith can match. The forge-masters who lead the city-states are first of all masters of this work.

The Engine

Section titled “The Engine”
  • Technology, Mechanisms, Metal, Recurrence

The Okoi are the foremost wielders of Technology, the continent’s newest-born polarity, expressed as Ren-driven machines: the golems, engines, shields, and singing blades that keep them a generation ahead of every rival forge.

The Walls

Section titled “The Walls”
  • Protection, Earth, Metal

A city-state is a fortress that became a city. Okoi martial tradition is built for the defense of fixed ground, founded on protection, fortification, and the same metalcraft that arms the rest of the continent.

The Hammer

Section titled “The Hammer”

The Okoi keep no banner of conquest, and the Accord flies no flag of its own. The shared mark of all six city-states is the smith’s hammer, the tool of the work that defines them, and the source of the Accord’s name.

The Accord’s instrument is sound. Copper bells, kept for the things that matter while iron is poured out by the ton, announce a local session of the Accord as they announce a dedication or a muster. When the bells of an Okoi city ring in the Accord’s sequence, the city knows that something has risen above the concerns of any one mountain.

Relations

Section titled “Relations”

The Accord’s standing with each nation is detailed in the Okoi City-States entry and summarized here.

  • Gankora is the most functional relationship the Accord holds. The United Anamor Nation claims no sovereignty over the three city-states in its territory and never has, and the two peoples, both native to the continent, deal as neighbors with standing.
  • Dosharen is transactional and managed city by city. Thirkoth’s position lets it work three houses at once, and the Accord does not negotiate Dosharen’s internal politics.
  • Lithywae is formal and stable. The Empire respects demonstrated strength, and Kolvrath has demonstrated it consistently.
  • Shejara is indirect and commercial. Kethvar’s river to Nashira carries Okoi metalwork into Shejaran trade, with no formal diplomacy attached.
  • The Keldar has worked with coastal Halvrak for generations, a professional respect between two organizations that have accurately measured each other.
