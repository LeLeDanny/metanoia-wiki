Guided Walkthrough: Who You Are Now

You have a lineage, an upbringing, and a Ren journey. Now you fill in the life between those points and land on who your character actually is at the moment play begins.

Experiences

Section titled “Experiences”

Experiences are the formative moments that shaped your character’s psychology. Roll 1 + (Realm x 3) times on the table below. At Realm 2, that’s 7 rolls.

Don’t just list them. Think about what each one meant, and how multiple results might connect. A character who rolled “betrayed someone,” “held a grudge,” and “joined a cause” has a compelling throughline.

Experiences also have a mechanical role during play. Each experience is assigned to one of your four values during the Values step below: it is the formative moment that led you to hold that belief. When an experience is relevant to the current scenario and you are using a polarity from that experience’s value in the resolution, you gain an experience boon. Only one experience can apply per resolution. See Boons & Complications for details.

Adventures

Section titled “Adventures”

Adventures are missions, jobs, or ordeals your character has already survived before play begins. Roll Realm + 1 times. At Realm 2, that’s 3 rolls.

Adventures are a great place to build connections with other players’ characters. If someone else rolled “protect a person,” maybe your character was that person.

Assigning Favor

Section titled “Assigning Favor”

You have 2 + Realm Favor points to assign across your relationships. Favor represents standing, leverage, and goodwill earned through shared history.

Taking -1 on a relationship gains you +1 to spend elsewhere.

Favor spent must be justified in fiction. If you have Favor with a merchant guild, it doesn’t help you with a temple unless there’s a clear narrative bridge. See Favor for the full rules.

Backstory

Section titled “Backstory”

Now that you have all the pieces, write a coherent backstory. Order the events, reconcile the tensions, and trace the decisions that brought your character to where play begins.

Focus on choices, not just events. What did they do, and what did that cost them?

Fears and Goals

Section titled “Fears and Goals”

Fears in this context are not phobias. They are the bad endings your character is trying to avoid: a sibling dying, being forgotten, losing control of their power, watching their nation fall.

Goals are often the mirror of fears. The character who fears their sister dying wants to cure her illness. The character who fears being forgotten wants to carve their name into history.

Keep both grounded in what came out of the lifepath.

Values

Section titled “Values”

Choose 4 values and rank them 1 to 4 (1 = highest priority). Values are the core of your character’s power in Metanoia. They determine which polarities you can develop, and how those polarities contribute to your dice pools through the Circulation passive.

The ranking also matters narratively. When two values conflict, the higher-ranked one usually wins. That choice is where character development happens.

d20VALUE BaselinesDefinition
1Love / SacrificeCompassion, mercy, loyalty, found family, sacrifice for others.
2FaithDevotion to something beyond yourself: divine, cosmic, fate, ideology.
3KnowledgeTruth, learning, understanding, curiosity.
4OrderLaw, codes, hierarchy, structure, discipline, authority.
5Justice / HonorRighting wrongs. Accountability. Consequences for harm.
6GrowthSelf-improvement, mastery, innovation, becoming more than you are.
7FreedomAutonomy, independence, rejecting control or imposed identity.
8PowerStrength, dominance, ambition, status, legacy.
9PeaceHarmony, de-escalation, balance, preservation of stability.
10TraditionAncestral ways, superstition, cultural continuity, inherited wisdom.
11BeautyAesthetic creation, expression, refinement, enjoyment, artistic pursuit.
12WealthYou value wealth, money, or assets.

Once you have your four values, go back through every experience you rolled and assign each one to a value. Ask: which belief did this moment push me toward? An experience can point toward a value even if the connection is oblique. A single experience can only be assigned to one value.

Not every value needs an experience assigned to it. Not every experience needs to connect neatly. The ones that do are the ones that will come alive at the table.

See Values for how values and their experiences shift during play.
