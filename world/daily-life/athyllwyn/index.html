Athyllwyn

There are two Lythiwaes.

There is the Lythiwae of the Imperial capital and the major sect compounds: formal, hierarchical, reputation-obsessed, governed by a code of conduct that has survived ten thousand years of recorded Nylvish history. And there is the Lythiwae of everywhere else: the boreal interior, the remote logging settlements, the frontier towns three weeks from the nearest sect checkpoint. Out there, the empire is a rumor. The law is whoever has the most Ren or the most bodies.

This split is not a failure of governance the empire is trying to fix. It is a stable condition.

Athyllwyn: The Capital

Section titled “Athyllwyn: The Capital”

Position and Geography

Section titled “Position and Geography”

Athyllwyn sits in a mountain pass near the northern end of the Kaelthar Range, where the peaks begin their descent into foothills on either side. The city is built into the pass itself: stone against stone, the range rising around it, the pass controlling transit between Lythiwae’s eastern and western coasts. A highland spring beneath the city feeds two rivers, one running west toward the bay at Orraeth, one running east toward the coast at Tyllym. Everything that moves between the empire’s two shores moves through here.

Beyond the northern edge of the pass, the mountains give way to foothills and then to boreal forest stretching outward until it is lost in distance or weather. In summer, the forest is an unbroken dark canopy. In winter, it goes grey-white, and the city sits above it like the last fixed point before the world becomes undifferentiated.

The city is cold. Even in summer, mornings require a heavy outer garment. In winter, the wind comes down from the mountains across the open northern exposure with nothing to deflect it. Visitors from Dosharen or Kyr’elian are frequently unprepared for how genuinely harsh the winters are.

Water comes from highland springs and snowmelt channeled into cisterns. Hot water for bathing is available throughout the city via Ren-heated cistern systems: one of the few obvious comforts in what is otherwise an austere capital.

Trade position: Athyllwyn is not a natural trade hub. Goods that reach the capital have been taxed and inspected and rerouted through layer after layer of sect and imperial checkpoint. What arrives is either luxury goods for the imperial court and sect compounds, or raw strategic materials from the interior. The city does not bustle. It presides.

Three Rings

Section titled “Three Rings”

Athyllwyn is organized in concentric rings, a deliberate design from early imperial city planning maintained and reinforced over centuries.

The Inner Ring holds the Imperial Palace compound of House Lyllynet, the Daemon Sect’s formal headquarters in the capital, and the official reception halls where foreign visitors and petitioners are processed. The architecture is the most formalized in the city: stone construction in basalt and pale granite, with vertical proportions that communicate permanence and authority. Some of the original Lydirian imperial structures remain, maintained but not replaced, because replacing them would erase visible proof of continuity. Access is governed by rank and documented purpose. The guards do not ask your name. They ask your standing.

The Middle Ring is where the functioning city lives: the Maelstrom Sect’s Athyllwyn outpost, the merchant quarters, the administrative offices of the imperial bureaucracy, and the residences of mid-to-high rank Nylvish families. Buildings are two to four stories in stone with timber-framed upper floors, pressed close together in residential sections. Shared walls are the norm. Privacy exists behind closed doors, not between buildings.

The Outer Ring is the working class and immigrant district: Nyl from the interior, Janim traders with temporary residence permits, Okoi craftspeople, and the Renak workforce that keeps the city physically running. Architecture here is timber-framed construction on stone foundations, less permanent-feeling than the inner rings. The outer ring has its own internal economy, its own social hierarchies independent of the imperial system, and its own methods for resolving disputes that do not involve the imperial courts.

Architecture: Austere Imperial

Section titled “Architecture: Austere Imperial”

Lythiwae’s architecture builds to communicate permanence, hierarchy, and Nylvish continuity. It does not build to celebrate public life.

General character: Vertical, stone-dominant, cold in color palette. Pale granite and dark basalt are the primary materials. Decorative elements exist, carved linear motifs on lintels and gate frames, but they are geometric and disciplined. The Nyl do not carve figures on public buildings. Figural representation is reserved for interior spaces and specific ritual contexts. From the street, a Nylvish building communicates only its scale and permanence.

Windows are narrow and deep-set, designed to retain heat more than admit light. Interiors are darker than Dosharen equivalents. Light inside comes from Ren-crystal lanterns. The quality of your crystals is a readable marker of status: in the inner ring, they burn a clean white-blue; in the outer ring, cheaper crystals burn warmer and less evenly.

The Hierarchy in Practice

Section titled “The Hierarchy in Practice”

The Nyl reputation system is not informal. It is structured, tracked, and consequential. Understanding it is not optional for anyone who wants to function in Athyllwyn.

Standing

Section titled “Standing”

Every Nyl in Athyllwyn has a social standing, understood by everyone who interacts with them. It is a combination of lineage rank, sect affiliation, demonstrated accomplishment, and accumulated reputation. It is not publicly posted anywhere, but it is known. The Nyl have maintained detailed lineage and deed records for thousands of years, and Athyllwyn’s scribal bureaucracy has records going back to the original Lydirian colonization of the Remnant Continent.

Your standing determines which checkpoints you pass freely, where you can be seated in formal dining and ceremony, whose greeting you initiate versus whose you wait to receive, whether a dispute you bring to the courts will be heard promptly or scheduled in six months, and what prices you pay for things.

The system is not perfectly rigid. Sect membership can elevate a low-lineage Rennim substantially. Demonstrated exceptional ability in a public context creates real upward movement.

Greeting Protocol

Section titled “Greeting Protocol”

Initiation: The lower-standing party initiates the greeting. If you are not sure who is lower, you wait. The more experienced person reads the hesitation and either signals you to initiate or initiates themselves as a courtesy. That signal is subtle: a slight forward inclination of the head. Missing it marks you as inexperienced. Catching it marks you as someone worth minimal patience.

The greeting itself: A formal bow, depth calibrated to the rank differential. A slight forward inclination for approximate equals, a significant bow for substantial differentials. The Nyl do not touch hands in formal greeting. Physical contact in formal context is a gesture of intimacy reserved for established relationships. Foreigners who extend a hand get a measured look and, if they are fortunate, a polite adjustment.

Eye contact: Sustained direct eye contact with a significantly higher-standing individual is inappropriate unless specifically invited. The correct posture is eye contact at greeting, brief and acknowledged, then eyes shifted slightly downward until the senior party signals conversation is proceeding. This is the piece most foreigners get wrong: in Dosharen or Kyr’elian contexts, direct eye contact signals respect. Here, it signals either a challenge or ignorance.

Emotional display: In public, most Nyl present as disaffected: not cold, not unfriendly, simply not expressive. Expressing significant emotion in formal public contexts marks low control and low cultivation. A Nyl who laughs loudly in a merchant negotiation is either making a calibrated point or has poor control. A foreigner who reads disaffected calm as unfriendliness is reading incorrectly.

The Debt System

Section titled “The Debt System”

The Nyl track social obligation with the same precision the Janim track financial debt. When someone of higher standing does something for you, that creates an obligation. The obligation is not immediately cashed in, usually. It accumulates. The person you owe knows it. You know it. Everyone who observed the exchange knows it.

The debt is paid through service, through deference in future disputes, through providing something the creditor needs that they might otherwise have had to acquire at cost. A player character navigating Athyllwyn will accumulate these debts as a natural consequence of needing things. The question is how their character relates to that web of obligation: accept it and play the system, pay debts as fast as they accrue, ignore the system and accept the cost of being marked as unreliable, or find a patron, which trades autonomy for security.

The Succession in Daily Life

Section titled “The Succession in Daily Life”

The faction politics of the Inner Ring do not stay in the Inner Ring.

In the merchant quarter, certain establishments are understood to cater to particular heir factions. This is never announced. It is simply known. Where you eat, which tea house you favor, which merchant gets your recurring business: in Athyllwyn’s current political climate, these are readable signals. The wrong association, noticed by the wrong person, can close a door that took years to open.

Political rumors reach the outer ring markets before they reach the imperial scribal service. By the time an Inner Ring decision becomes official record, the outer ring has had a version of it for two days. The version is not always accurate. It is always faster.

Checkpoint behavior has grown inconsistent. Guards who once waved through familiar faces now ask more questions. Being seen as lenient with someone aligned to the wrong faction is a risk nobody wants to carry. The result is a city where movement is slightly more friction-laden than it was five years ago, in ways that are difficult to articulate but impossible to miss if you live here.

The heir factions have developed unofficial identifying markers: the way a sash is worn, a cord color on a courier bag, a particular seal on a letter that has nothing to do with its sender’s house. People in the middle ring learn to read these. People in the outer ring learn from watching people in the middle ring.

The debt system has become more fraught than usual. A debt owed to someone whose faction loses the succession may not be worth collecting once the new reign is settled. People who have spent decades maintaining careful obligation networks are now quietly recalculating which debts to pay down, which to let drift, which new obligations are worth acquiring. The uncertainty does not produce paralysis. The Nyl are too practiced at navigating hierarchy for that. It produces a city where everyone is, behind the composed exterior, running numbers.

Food & Drink

Section titled “Food & Drink”

Climate-Driven Eating

Section titled “Climate-Driven Eating”

Lythiwae’s boreal territory shapes its food culture completely. The growing season is short. Preserved food is the foundation of the diet. Fermentation, cold-smoking, brining, and fat preservation are not artisanal techniques; they are survival methods refined over thousands of years into a genuine culinary tradition.

Staples: Root vegetables stored through winter in deep-cellar systems, preserved fish from interior rivers (the cold, fast rivers of the boreal forest are rich with fish), cured meats from large game, dense dark breads made with imported or highland-grown grain, dairy from cold-adapted cattle.

Fermented things are everywhere: fermented fish sauce used as a seasoning the way you would use salt, fermented cabbage and root vegetables as preservation and side dish, a fermented grain drink lower in alcohol than beer and consumed with food more than for intoxication. Heavier drinking happens in private or in specific ceremonial contexts, not in public eating houses.

Fat is a nutrient and a preservation medium. Animal fat is used generously in most cooked food. A Dosharen visitor used to olive-oil cooking might find Lythiwae food rich and heavy. A local eating Dosharen food might find it thin.

Meat is the status food. Large game from the interior, elk, massive cold-adapted boar, and large forest deer, reserved for formal meals and high-standing households. In the capital, the ability to serve fresh game in winter signals either the standing to access it through imperial allocation or the connections to get it through informal channels.

Hot food is ritualized. A meal served warm in winter is a statement of care or status. Public eating houses have central hearths that do most of the cooking, and the warmth of the room is part of what you are paying for.

The Tea Ritual

Section titled “The Tea Ritual”

The Nyl drink tea with the same cultural gravity that the Janim drink wine.

The high-quality product that reaches Athyllwyn’s inner ring households is pressed and aged, producing a deep, dark brew with a particular bitterness the Nyl consider the mark of quality. Cheaper tea in the outer ring is a cruder product, dried leaf rather than pressed cake, which brews faster and rougher. You can read someone’s resources and connections by the quality of their tea.

In formal contexts: Tea is prepared at a low table, deliberately and visibly. Grinding or breaking the pressed cake, the specific temperature of water, the steep time. Rushing the preparation is a social mistake. Being visibly impatient while watching it is worse. The first cup is served to the highest-standing guest. Drinking before everyone is served is an egregious breach. The ritual is a structured demonstration that you are willing to subordinate immediate desire to correct procedure, which is, in miniature, what the hierarchy requires of everyone.

In informal and outer ring contexts: The ritual is stripped down, not eliminated. Even in a rough frontier town eating house, tea is poured for the highest-standing person first. The cup quality degrades. The tea quality degrades. The ritual persists, because it is not really about tea.

Formal Meals

Section titled “Formal Meals”

Seating is established before guests arrive. You do not find your own seat. A household member directs you to your position, calculated relative to every other guest’s standing. Getting seating wrong is an insult to the displaced person and a reflection on the host’s organizational competence.

Multiple small dishes are served sequentially. The number of courses is proportional to the formality and the host’s resources. The minimum for a significant formal meal is five courses. Imperial-level formal dinners can exceed twenty.

Conversation protocol: Discussion of substantive matters, business, disputes, and politics, is not done during the meal itself. The meal is for social calibration and the performance of goodwill. Substantive conversation happens after, in a separate space, after tea. A foreigner who raises business at the table gets their point acknowledged with a polite noncommittal sound and the topic smoothly moved aside.

Sect Life in the City

Section titled “Sect Life in the City”

The martial sects are not background institutions in Athyllwyn. They are present, visible, and consequential.

Maelstrom Sect members are common enough in the city that their presence is unremarkable. A Maelstrom cultivator in sect colors, deep grey-blue with a silver spiral mark at the shoulder, walking the middle ring streets, draws no particular attention. They are part of the city’s texture.

Frozen Flower Sect members in Athyllwyn are rare. Their compound is far north, and their presence in the capital usually means a specific mission or a formal audience. When they appear, people notice. Not because of fear, necessarily, but because of rarity. Their color is white with silver chaining detail at the cuffs.

Daemon Sect are almost never seen in public in their operational capacity. When they are visible at an imperial ceremony or moving through the Inner Ring on documented business, they pull a particular kind of silence. Not the silence of fear exactly, though fear is present. The silence of people who have correctly identified something that operates by different rules and are giving it room.

Celestial Sword Sect members in Athyllwyn are rare and politically complicated. Their jurisdiction is Ingridrid-polarity corruption. The Imperial Dynasty tolerates their presence because refusing to would be a statement nobody wants to make.

Sect Recruitment

Section titled “Sect Recruitment”

The Maelstrom Sect recruits openly in Athyllwyn’s outer ring. A cultivation scout, usually a Realm 3 member, walks the outer ring, watches the street fights and informal competitions that happen despite being technically illegal, and identifies young Rennim worth approaching. The offer: sect training, resources to advance Realm, a path out of the outer ring’s social ceiling. The cost: years of your life, sect obligation, and the certainty that the sect will use you for things you did not get to choose.

Some families who have produced Rennim members across generations treat sect recruitment as an honor and a relief. Some treat it as a threat dressed as an opportunity. Both responses are rational.

GM: The Daemon Sect and the Rewera

The Reweran Anamor occupy a reservation in northern Lythiwae. The empire could not govern them and could not remove them, so it contained them: a bounded territory, controlled access, and a program that has been running for generations that most of Athyllwyn’s court does not discuss directly.

Selected Rewera are compelled to produce children with Nyl. The children born of these pairings are taken and raised as Daemon Sect initiates. They do not choose this. The sect is not something they join. It is what they are made into from birth, shaped by training that begins before they are old enough to understand what they are being shaped for.

The process that produces a Daemon is not something the empire publicizes. The sect’s fearsome reputation is maintained precisely because no one outside a small circle of imperial officials knows its origin. What the court presents as Lythiwae’s most disciplined and loyal instrument is, at its foundation, built on coercion.

Aleswyr is one of these.

Reweran individuals outside the reservation are noticed immediately in Athyllwyn. Their biology is visible and legible. The Daemon Sect handles any unsanctioned Reweran presence in the city. The handling is opaque to everyone outside the sect.

The Renak

Section titled “The Renak”

The Renak are the physical foundation of Athyllwyn. Stone maintenance for the Inner Ring’s infrastructure, portage and delivery through the merchant quarter, water management for the cistern systems, fuel for the outer ring eating houses: the city requires continuous labor to function, and the Renak provide almost all of it. Their absence would be visible within a day. This is not something the imperial hierarchy acknowledges publicly.

Permits and Checkpoints

Section titled “Permits and Checkpoints”

Renak who work in the Inner or Middle Ring need a work permit documenting their employment and their employer’s affiliation. Without it, they cannot enter during work hours. The permits are issued by imperial clerks who are overworked and underpaid, which has produced a gray market in expedited processing that outer ring Renak communities have learned to navigate. Knowing who to approach, and what it costs, is practical knowledge passed through families and neighborhoods rather than anything official.

The Outer Ring Community

Section titled “The Outer Ring Community”

The outer ring’s Renak population has its own internal hierarchy, entirely separate from the imperial standing system. Seniority matters. Reputation for reliability matters. The ability to help someone find work, navigate a permit problem, or connect a family newly arrived from the interior to an employer: these are the currencies. A Renak elder who has lived in the outer ring for forty years and knows every foreman in the merchant quarter has standing that does not appear anywhere in the imperial record and is more practically useful than what most middle ring residents possess.

Maelstrom Connections

Section titled “Maelstrom Connections”

Outer ring Renak families who have a Maelstrom member know what that connection is worth. The member has standing. The family is still Renak. The member can intercede, quietly, in ways the hierarchy would not permit if stated openly. Everyone in the relevant neighborhoods knows which families have that connection. Being owed a favor by one of them is the kind of asset that does not go into writing.

Renak Children Who Manifest Ren

Section titled “Renak Children Who Manifest Ren”

It happens. A Renak child who develops Ren ability occupies a structurally awkward position: no longer fully Renak by the system’s logic, but without the lineage documentation or sect affiliation that would give them formal standing. The Maelstrom scouts watch the outer ring specifically for this. Some families treat sect recruitment as a relief, a path upward for a child who would otherwise be locked into Renak life regardless of their ability. Some treat it as a loss. The child who leaves for sect training is leaving behind the community that raised them for an institution that will remake them entirely.

The Interior

Section titled “The Interior”

Outside Athyllwyn and the major sect compounds, Lythiwae operates by different rules.

How Bribery Works

Section titled “How Bribery Works”

In the interior, bribery is not a crime. It is how things get done. Checkpoint guards on interior roads are paid a state salary everyone knows is insufficient. They supplement it through a “passage fee,” calibrated by what you are carrying and where you are going. There is a local market rate. Charging dramatically above it causes problems, because travelers talk and checkpoint guards who are too aggressive get quietly sorted out by sect patrols that care more about the roads working than about passage fees.

You pay, you move. You refuse to pay, you sit. Nobody pretends the formal law applies. Anyone with Ryc or Al to spend can move through the interior regardless of standing, which is the thing the formal hierarchy cannot tolerate publicly but depends on practically.

Dispute Resolution

Section titled “Dispute Resolution”

Disputes in the interior are settled by demonstrated force or by informal arbitration. The arbitrators are people with local reputation: usually a high-Realm cultivator who has chosen to stay in the interior rather than seek a seat of power, or a Reweran tribal elder whose neutrality is respected.

The Nylvish reputation system persists in the interior but functions differently. Lineage records are not current this far out. What matters is what you have done here, recently, in front of people who are still alive. A Nyl with a ten-thousand-year lineage record who arrives in an interior town and cannot demonstrate anything useful is lower standing than a mixed-lineage Rennim who killed a monster threatening the local food supply last winter.

Daily Rhythms

Section titled “Daily Rhythms”

The Bell System

Section titled “The Bell System”

Athyllwyn uses a bell system for timekeeping. Bell towers in each ring sound on the major divisions of the day. The bells are Ren-maintained: they sound at precisely correct intervals, without variability. The inner ring bells are louder than the middle ring bells, which are louder than the outer ring bells. This is engineering. But the fact that the imperially-maintained bells are loudest nearest the palace has taken on symbolic weight regardless.

The morning bell is when the city opens. Eating houses begin service. The inner ring checkpoint begins its formal watch rotation. Outer ring markets set up.

The evening bell signals formal business to close. What continues after is informal: private dining, sect compound internal activities, the outer ring’s night economy.

The night bell signals curfew for certain categories of people: outer ring residents with conditional permits, foreign visitors with documented residences, and anyone whose checkpoint pass specifies night restrictions. It is not enforced aggressively, but if you are stopped after the night bell without authorization, you are answering questions about it.

Work and Rest

Section titled “Work and Rest”

The Nylvish do not have a concept of leisure time that maps neatly onto what a Dosharen resident would recognize. Cultivation practice is not separate from life; it is life, for those who are Rennim. Maintaining standing requires ongoing demonstration of worth. Rest is legitimate and necessary and taken, but not celebrated. A Nyl who tells you they spent the afternoon resting is giving you information about their health situation or their recent exertion level. They are not bragging about their free time.

The Renak population works on a more recognizable cycle: labor occupies the majority of the day, and what exists outside it is practical recovery and social connection. The outer ring has a lively informal culture that the imperial structure largely ignores, because it produces the labor the city runs on and disrupting it would be counterproductive.

Outsiders in Lythiwae

Section titled “Outsiders in Lythiwae”

A Janim in Athyllwyn

Section titled “A Janim in Athyllwyn”

A Janim in Athyllwyn is navigating active cultural hostility at the institutional level and mixed individual response at the street level. The imperial court’s relationship with Dosharen is cold, and Nylvish historical memory of the Janim-Nyl enmity has not fully metabolized. The Nyl’s version of that history is different from the Janim version, and both involve grievance.

On the street, most outer ring Nyl deal with enough Janim traders that the hostility is muted by familiarity. The Janim trader who has been coming to the same outer ring market stall quarterly for three years is not the abstraction that historical enmity requires. The institutional hostility and individual familiarity coexist, and the gap between them is where Janim characters in Lythiwae actually live.

Being an Outsider Here

Section titled “Being an Outsider Here”

A non-Nyl player character in Athyllwyn is functioning in a city where their cultural defaults are wrong. The greeting protocol is different. The emotional register is different. The food is different. The relationship to time and rest is different. The bribery system that works everywhere else on the continent does not operate the same way here: attempting to bribe an inner ring guard is not a negotiation, it is an insult that marks you as someone who does not understand that the inner ring runs on standing, not cash.

Sensory Palette

Section titled “Sensory Palette”

Sound

Section titled “Sound”

Athyllwyn is quieter than its population would suggest: not silent, but measured. The loudest public spaces are the outer ring markets in the morning. After the opening bell, the city sounds formal: footsteps on stone, the rare raised voice that carries further than it should because everything around it is so composed.

Maelstrom training at the sect compound generates controlled bursts of Ren activity that Rennim in the middle ring can feel as pressure changes. Most middle ring residents have learned to ignore it. New arrivals sometimes come to a dead stop in the street.

The inner ring checkpoint change-of-watch is precisely timed, preceded by a short structured auditory signal. Visitors who hear it for the first time sometimes startle, because it is precisely on the hour and there is no anticipation.

Cold and Light

Section titled “Cold and Light”

The dominant sensory experience of Athyllwyn is cold, even in summer. Surfaces are cool to the touch. The stone of the inner ring holds cold the way a cave does. In winter, breath is visible from late morning through midday. The Ren-crystal lighting gives everything a cooler tone than firelight; interiors feel illuminated rather than warm.

In summer, when the boreal forest below the city is in full canopy, the reflected green light from the treeline tints the northern-facing walls of the outer ring in the afternoon. Locals do not comment on it. Visitors find it strange: a cold stone city with green-tinted light on one face, as though the forest is watching.
