The Sand Swept Princes

House Dajimir, the veiled rulers of Shejara and the only Highborn house on the continent that governs a nation without a council, a parliament, or any competing noble body to answer to. The house is Janim Highborn, carrying both the Janim law of Elasticity and the Daruner blood that, across the Remnant Continent, is read as the right to rule. House Dajimir takes that reading further than anyone: they hold that governance itself requires Daruner blood, and they built a whole country on the principle.

To the outside world the Princes are barely a presence at all. They receive envoys with perfect courtesy from behind the veils of their office, say almost nothing of substance, and send them home with nothing. What the continent fears about Shejara has little to do with anything the Princes have shown it, and everything to do with what the silence and the vanishing leave room to imagine.

Overview

Section titled “Overview”
TypeSingle-house oligarchy; the ruling Princes of Shejara
HouseDajimir, Janim Highborn (Elasticity and Coalescence)
SeatJadir’ishet
StructureSeven lines, rotating the throne every seven years
Public faceVeiled. The office is shown; the person is not.
ReachShejara

House Dajimir is not a dynasty in the ordinary sense. Rather than passing power down a single bloodline, the house divided itself centuries ago into seven lines, and the throne rotates between them on a fixed schedule. At any time one line reigns and the other six advise and wait. The Princes who make up these lines are the government of Shejara entire: there is no body above them, beside them, or beneath them with a claim on power.

The arrangement is old, rigid, and deliberately resistant to change. It has held Shejara in one house’s grip for as long as Shejara has been a nation, and the Princes intend it to go on holding.

Values

Section titled “Values”
ValueWhy
OrderThe seven-line rotation is the purest expression of order on the continent: a system built to run regardless of who holds the throne, immune to performance, preference, or emergency. Process is the point. The structure outranks any person inside it.
TraditionThe rotation order was fixed at the founding and has never moved. House Dajimir is also the spiritual seat of the Order of the Divines, keeper of the oldest temples and the codified faith. The Princes rule by the weight of how long they have ruled.
PowerNot ambition, but possession. House Dajimir holds an entire country uncontested and means to keep it so. Their guiding belief, that governance authority must follow Daruner blood, is the claim they once partitioned an empire to defend, and the legitimacy on which everything else rests.

Philosophy

Section titled “Philosophy”

House Dajimir believes that the right to govern is carried in the blood, and that Daruner blood specifically is the only legitimate foundation for authority. Far from a doctrine of convenience, it is the principle the house broke the Erbaune Empire over, refusing a reform that would have let citizens of sufficient standing sit on the imperial council without Highborn heritage. Where Dosharen accepted earned standing alongside inherited blood, Shejara would not, and the two parted rather than reconcile. The Princes consider their country the half of Erbaune that kept faith with the truth.

Everything else about the Princes is preservation. The rotation exists to keep any single line from gathering enough power to seize the house, because a house that turns on itself is a house that can be taken. The isolation exists to keep the wider world disinterested: a Shejara understood to be poor, proud, and remote is a Shejara no one bothers to force open, and the Princes have spent centuries cultivating exactly that impression. They do not want the continent’s attention. They want its indifference, and they have purchased it with courtesy and distance for generations.

What the Princes guard, they guard absolutely. The veils, the closed interior, the envoys received warmly and told nothing, the patrols that turn travelers back at the edge of the deep desert: all of it serves the same end. The Princes have decided that survival lies in being underestimated, and they protect that misjudgment more carefully than they protect anything a stranger could see.

The Seven Lines

Section titled “The Seven Lines”

House Dajimir is divided into seven lines, each a branch of the house with its own head, its own genealogical records, and its own domain of governance. The throne passes between them in a fixed order set at the founding.

LineDomain
VareshDiplomatic protocol and historical records. The oldest line, with the longest institutional memory.
KeshinCommerce and resource allocation.
NamiraReligious oversight and the Order’s temple networks. Traditional stewards of the faith.
OthajMaritime affairs and coastal licensing. The most outward-facing line.
SarimMilitary and border security. Currently Reigning.
TirethTreasury and foreign trade permits. Next in rotation.
ImsharDesert survey and ruin administration. The oldest relationship with the deep interior.

Each line holds the throne for exactly seven years, a span called a Reign, before passing it to the next. The full cycle of all seven takes forty-nine years, so a citizen sees several changes of power in a lifetime but watches the wheel turn fully only once a generation. The order never changes. A line that governs badly is replaced on schedule all the same, and a line that governs well cannot keep the throne a day longer. The system was never built to produce good rulers. It was built so that no line could ever become strong enough to break it.

The Reigning Prince and the Council

Section titled “The Reigning Prince and the Council”

The head of the line currently in power is the Reigning Prince, who holds executive authority over Shejara. The heads of the other six lines form the Advisory Council as the Seated Princes, debating the throne openly and exercising their own functional domains while they wait.

The Council can overrule the Reigning Prince only by unanimous vote, all six Seated Princes agreeing at once. A single dissenting line lets the throne proceed, and getting six proud lines to agree on anything is rare enough that the Reigning Prince governs with what amounts to near-absolute authority. The real check is subtler. Three lines acting together, withholding the cooperation of their domains and making their displeasure felt within the house, can make a Reign miserable without ever casting a vote. A Reigning Prince spends seven years balancing the throne’s power against the patience of the lines who will outlast it.

The seven-year limit sharpens everything. Whatever a Prince cannot accomplish in their Reign waits at least forty-two more years before their line holds the throne again. Urgency and patience live side by side in every line: act now, because the throne is brief, and endure, because the wheel always comes back around.

Becoming a Prince

Section titled “Becoming a Prince”

When a line’s Reign approaches, the line chooses its Prince from among its eligible members by its own internal election, fielding whoever it judges its strongest candidate for the era to come. The choice is the line’s alone, and the rest of the house has no voice in it.

This freedom carries a brutal stake. By the No-Heir Clause, a line that cannot produce a Prince when its rotation arrives loses the throne to the next line in order and forfeits its following rotation as well, a fourteen-year exile from power rather than seven. The prospect of missing two consecutive Reigns turns succession planning into an obsession. The lines keep exacting genealogies and negotiate marriages decades in advance, all to be certain that when the wheel turns to them, there is someone ready to be elected and veiled.

The Veil

Section titled “The Veil”

The Princes rule from behind veils, and the world treats with an office rather than a face. In public ceremony and before foreign envoys, a Prince appears masked and veiled in the regalia of their line, addressed by line and title and never by personal name. Which individual wears the veil of a given line is a matter the house keeps to itself. Inside House Dajimir, identities and genealogies are recorded with obsessive precision. Outside it, the Princes are deliberately faceless.

The veil serves the same purpose as everything else the Princes do. A ruler with no public face cannot be courted, blackmailed, singled out, or struck at, and a foreign power that cannot say who truly governs Shejara cannot easily reach them. The continent deals with the Sand Swept Princes and comes away unable to describe a single one of them.

The Closed Country

Section titled “The Closed Country”

Shejara permits coastal trade, licensed and limited, through specific ports. It permits almost nothing else. Foreign merchants who try to move inland past the authorized zones are turned back by border patrols whose courtesy is total and whose firmness is absolute, and travelers who push into the deep interior anyway do not reliably come back.

This is the whole of what the outside world actually knows: the desert is harsh, the Princes are remote, and those who go too far are not seen again. No confirmed account explains the vanishings, and into that silence the continent has poured a century of rumor. The most persistent of these is that the Princes uncovered something beneath the sands and guard it with their lives. The Princes neither confirm nor deny it. They have found that a fear without a shape frightens more thoroughly than any answer could, and costs them nothing to maintain.

GM: What the Princes Actually Guard

The vanishings are real and the rumor is closer to the truth than the rumor-mongers know. Line Imshar found Marathat ruins in the deep southwest generations ago, and at their heart a single portable artifact that draws water from far underground and turns desert into farmland over years. It is the source of Shejara’s quiet food independence and the reason the border closed. Because the artifact can be moved, its discovery would make Shejara a target for every food-insecure nation, every Ren institution, and every ambitious king on the continent. The closed interior, the licensed corridors, the faceless courtesy, and Line Sarim’s interior patrols that return intruders to the coast with a story (or, when that fails, do not return them at all) exist to protect this one thing. The full account lives in the Shejara entry.

The lines do not share this knowledge equally. Imshar, Sarim, and Tireth hold full operational knowledge. Namira knows enough to grasp the theological problem. Varesh, Keshin, and Othaj know only that something significant sits in the southwest and that they are not being told what. Should a line outside the inner circle ever take the throne and demand full disclosure, the structure that has held since the partition will be tested.

Polarities

Section titled “Polarities”

House Dajimir is Janim Highborn, and its Princes wield both of their inherited laws.

The Ruling Blood

Section titled “The Ruling Blood”
  • Elasticity, Coalescence

The Janim Elasticity gives the line its endurance, the body and Ren that return to form under sustained strain, a fitting trait for a house that governs by outlasting. The Daruner Coalescence is the foundation of Highborn authority across the continent: the power to seal, bind, and contain, and the basis of the legal oaths, sealed records, and bound infrastructure through which a Highborn house rules. To House Dajimir, Coalescence is more than a tool of governance. It is the proof of their right to govern at all.

Beyond the lineage laws, Shejaran Rennim trained in the Princes’ service tend toward the polarities the desert rewards: Earth, Sand, and the working of Water that a nation built on a single channel cannot live without.

History

Section titled “History”

Shejara and Dosharen were once a single realm, the Erbaune Empire. The empire fractured over a question of governance: whether citizens of sufficient standing and knowledge, lacking Highborn blood, could be permitted to sit on the imperial council. House Dajimir opposed it on principle, holding that governance authority must follow Daruner blood. Neither faction would yield, and after decades of paralysis the empire partitioned into two successor states, the eastern bloc becoming Dosharen and House Dajimir taking Shejara. Both nations record the parting as a dignified disagreement between two valid readings of Erbaune tradition.

The seven-line system predates the partition. House Dajimir divided itself into its rotating lines centuries ago, choosing a structure that no single ambition could capture over the simpler dynasty that every other Highborn house kept. It is the oldest part of what the Princes are, and the part they will least permit to change.

GM: Why the Empire Really Split

The governance argument was sincere, but Line Imshar had been surveying the deep desert for decades and had already found enough to know it wanted no imperial audit rights applied to the interior. A reform expanding the council was a reform increasing the number of eyes that would eventually see the survey reports. Dajimir pushed for partition with unusual urgency, and the eastern bloc let them go cheaply, believing the desert a resource drain they were glad to shed. See Shejara for the full account.

Religion

Section titled “Religion”

House Dajimir is the spiritual seat of the Order of the Divines. The faith was codified into its organized form by Dajimir-sponsored scholars and spread outward from Shejara, and the oldest temples on the continent stand in Jadir’ishet. Line Namira has stewarded the faith’s institutional structure for generations. This gives the Princes a soft authority over the Order they have never formally claimed and never relinquished. Yrialin exists in Shejara only among scholars and city-dwellers, with no cathedral and no chapter house, which is exactly how the Princes prefer a rival faith: institutionally weak if not almost non-existent.

Relations

Section titled “Relations”

Shejara’s standing with each nation is detailed in the Shejara entry and summarized here.

  • Dosharen is a frozen distance with deep roots. The two are the halves of a sundered empire, and the border carries no convoys and no conflict, only envoys exchanged in courteous futility. Dosharen’s Highborn houses have probed Shejara for what it hides and learned nothing.
  • Lithywae is managed with the same distance. The Nylvish empire sees no value in desert and has been given no reason to look closer.
  • The Hammer Accord trades through the coast. The Okoi city-state of Kethvar sits in northern Shejara and sends its metalwork south to Nashira, and House Dajimir has never made a formal approach to it, which the Okoi read as a boon.
  • Coastal trade with Kyr’elian and others is permitted in licensed, limited form through specific ports, and never inland.
